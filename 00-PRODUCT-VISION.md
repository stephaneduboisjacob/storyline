# 00 — Product Vision

## The one sentence

> **We call Grandma every week; her stories become the family podcast — and, only with her recorded consent, an echo after.**

Grandma owns nothing, installs nothing, learns nothing. She answers her phone. That is the entire user interface for the most important user.

## The two products

### Product 1 — StoryLine Capture (while she lives)

A friendly, patient, curious voice calls her every week at a time she chose. It remembers what she said last week. It asks about the farm, the war years, Roland, the tomato story, how she met Grandpa. Every call is recorded, transcribed, and kept.

The family receives:

- **The private podcast** — the best cuts of her voice, every week, in their podcast app.
- **The transcript library** — searchable, organized by person, place, and era.
- **The book** — once a year, her year of calls becomes a hardcover ("Mom's Book, Vol. 3").
- **Company for her** — the calls themselves are the retention engine. She *looks forward* to them.

Capture alone is a complete, honest, sellable product. If the Echo never existed, StoryLine would still be worth $15/month to every family with an aging parent.

### Product 2 — The Echo (after, optional, consent-gated)

If — and only if — she recorded a consent manifesto while alive, the family may speak with an echo after she's gone: her voice, her speech patterns, her actual remembered stories, retrieved from her archive. Framed always as a portrait that talks, never a person returned. Full spec in `07-ECHO-SPEC.md`.

## The five pillars

| Pillar | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Alive-first** | The product's center of gravity is the living woman and her weekly call. Death features are optional endings, not the marketing. |
| **Phone-first** | Telephony only for her. No app, no upload, no "digital literacy." The only capture product a 92-year-old uses weekly for years. |
| **Consent-first** | She decides what is recorded, who hears it, and what may happen after. Recorded in her voice, while she's alive. See `01`. |
| **Family-owned** | The archive belongs to the family. Full export in standard formats, any time, forever — including if the company dies. |
| **No ransom** | Capture is a subscription while she lives. The book is one-time. The Echo is a one-time legacy license. Nothing bills monthly against grief. |

## Market landscape (honest)

| Competitor | What they do | What they don't do |
|---|---|---|
| **StoryWorth** (~$99/yr) | Emails Grandma a weekly writing prompt; she writes replies; year-end hardcover book. Huge, proven demand. | Requires her to *write*. No voice. No phone. Nothing after. |
| **HereAfter AI** | App-based recorded interviews; family browses memories. | Requires her to use an app. Griefbot-lite features exist. |
| **StoryFile** | Professional video interviews, interactive memorial video. | Expensive, studio-flavored, one-shot — not a weekly ritual. |
| **Remento** | Voice recordings via device/app → book. | Hardware/app friction again. |
| **Chinese AI-resurrection studios** | Post-death avatars and voice clones, growing industry. | After-only, consent often ambiguous, no living relationship. |

**Our wedge:** everyone else demands she *does something* (write, tap, sit for an interview). We only demand she *answers her phone* — and the weekly call builds the relationship, the corpus, and the consent moment as byproducts of a call she enjoys. Telephony capture at weekly cadence is the moat; the consent manifesto is the trust; the book is the Christmas gift.

**Ideal first customer:** a daughter, 45–60, bilingual or French-first, pays the bill, sets everything up in 10 minutes, and lives 400 km from her mother. The mother, 75–90, has a landline or basic cellphone, tells great stories, and is slightly lonely on Tuesday afternoons.

## What StoryLine is NOT

- **Not a medical alert service.** No emergency response promise, ever. If she is distressed, the protocol is gentle care plus an optional family notification flag (see `03`, Distress protocol).
- **Not therapy.** Not a companion robot sold as a cure for loneliness. It is a storyteller with a microphone and very good manners.
- **Not a resurrection.** The Echo is labeled, framed, and bounded as an echo.
- **Not a data mine.** No ad tech, no "anonymized insights," no resale of a single second of her voice. The corpus is the family's treasure, not our inventory.

## Success metrics (first year)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Weekly call answer rate (healthy elder) | > 70% |
| Families still subscribed at month 3 | > 80% |
| Calls where she speaks > 60% of the time | > 75% |
| Book orders per active family per year | > 0.5 |
| Families who say "the calls themselves are worth it" | majority in exit interviews |
| Echo activations with clean consent | 100% of all activations, no exceptions |

## Brand voice (how we talk)

Warm, plain, a little old-fashioned. Short sentences. French-first in Québec, natural bilingual everywhere. We never say: "leverage," "digital immortality," "AI-powered legacy solution." We say: "the calls," "her book," "her stories," "keeping her voice."
